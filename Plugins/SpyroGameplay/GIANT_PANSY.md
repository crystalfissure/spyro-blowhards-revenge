# Giant Pansy — Jacques

Place `/Game/OT_Ports/S1/S1_Enemies/Home_04_Dream_Weavers/05_Jacques/Giant_Pansy_BP` in a level. Select its `GiantPansyBehavior` component and enable **Roaming** for the mobile variant. The default is stationary. `Pursuer` permits a target override; otherwise the component finds the local Spyro. No navigation mesh or patrol spline is required.

The Blueprint uses the existing damageable, item dropper, gem counting, and checkpoint contracts. It has one hit point, resists Ram, and dies to Burn. The default reward is one five-value blue gem (`Gem_Blueish_Purple_BP`); `Items_to_Drop` remains editable per placement. The original Jacques scene has eleven Pansies: seven drop five-value gems, three drop two-value gems, and one drops a ten-value gem. Two of those placements enable the roaming flag.

Reference: model class 46 and the NTSC Jacques overlay, pinned to [spyro-1 revision 46b51d3](https://github.com/TheMobyCollective/spyro-1/tree/46b51d3a5c34dd2585b4fe1de74ac9b74c76390d). All 8,808 instruction words in the researched update function match the supplied local disc. Model headers supply seven clip lengths `[34,15,46,9,9,8,13]`, rates `[11,16,16,16,16,16,16]`, the two sound samples, frame sound cues, and collision groups. The two source FBXs have separate vertex rigs; both meshes, skeletons, textures, and all seven clips are saved with the enemy.

The simulation runs at 30 Hz independently of rendering. Stationary Pansies turn toward Spyro and attack within 1,800 original units. They return to idle after their attack and count down the original 160-unit cooldown by four per idle tick. Roaming Pansies reveal within 8,192 units when Spyro faces them and the player's camera also faces them. Reveal frame 3 starts the chase. Chase speed is 100 units/tick, return speed is 90, and facing changes by at most four angle units/tick. They attack inside 1,200 units, then return home; looking away for the original 40-unit timer, blocked ground movement, or exceeding 10,240 units from home also triggers return. Home arrival uses the original 128-unit tolerance. Idle/attack facing uses seven angle units with a five-unit dead zone.

Flower contact uses original collision group 0: two radius-400 spheres bound to original vertices 0 and 1. It activates on attack clip 1 frames 4–14 and roaming attack clip 3 frames 4–8. Positive interpolation progress selects the next original frame for the collision group. Contact adapts the spheres to Spyro's capsule and requires a clear physical path. The shared Normal Damage reaction supplies player feedback and knockback; a successful hit is limited to once per attack. The overlay's separate unconditional Spyro velocity writes are not replayed against a distant or obstructed player.

Death hard-selects clip 4, launches with horizontal speed 400 and vertical speed 170, reduces horizontal speed by 12 and vertical speed by 16 per tick, and finishes when the clip completes. The launch direction follows the original bounded away/Spyro-heading blend. A small `P_DustCloud` puff appears on disappearance. Checkpoint reset restores home, health, visibility, collisions, the appropriate idle rig, and reward guards; emitted gems remain registered with the shared dropper for cleanup.

Unreal adaptations: terrain movement uses capsule sweeps and ground probes; the original blocking capsule is approximated by a body box and thin charge sensor. The flower is excluded from the blocking body. Live lower-body vertices anchor the dump's pose origins to terrain so neither rig floats during idle or locomotion. Death retains its launch and animated vertical motion. Frozen, fear, reset, and paused states suspend combat. The original additional `0x80000` defeat flag has no separate project damage mapping. Camera gating uses the targeted player's camera when a player controller is available. Audio uses the existing Town Square attenuation.

`OnEnemySignal` exposes `AttackCommitted` (clip 1 or 3) and `ResetCompleted`. AttackCommitted identifies wind-up, not a landed hit; listeners should not apply additional damage. Debug fields expose state, clip/frame, simulation tick count, attack/hit counts, gem count, and sound history.

Validation: UE4.27.2 editor and Win64 runtime builds pass, and the installed DLLs match the isolated build outputs. The 29 core PIE checks pass at 60 FPS; all 31 extended checks pass at 20 FPS. Coverage includes the fixed simulation clock, charge immunity, flower wind-up/contact/sounds, stationary behavior, flame death, rig switching, one reward, checkpoint restoration, roaming movement/attack/return, and physical-wall filtering. The fixture uses the saved enemy and actual Spyro/shared components, disables unsolicited player attack-volume overlaps, and restores game time after player damage. It logs existing missing-controller errors in the player's swimming graph. Freeze/fear guards and the real player-camera gate were reviewed in code but not exercised by this headless suite.

Bull, Toreador, Sleeping Dog, Toasty, Snow Gnorc, and Giant Pansy Blueprints all compile. Rendered stationary/roaming meshes were inspected for textures and ground alignment. All 20 new assets have Windows cook output. The final iterative cook reports zero errors and one unused MLSDK warning; its initial dependency cook reports 83 existing project warnings.

Research, import scripts, build hosts, renders, and disposable PIE fixtures live in `Desktop/Scripts/giant_pansy_research`, outside the game repository. Production maps are not edited by this implementation. A controller playthrough and packaged executable launch remain separate validation steps.
