# Sleeping Dog and Toasty

UE4.27.2 assets live in `/Game/OT_Ports/S1/S1_Enemies/Home_00_Artisans/04_Toasty`:

- `Sleeping_Dog_BP`: sleeping, pouncing, two-flame dog; one green gem on defeat.
- `Toasty_BP`: three-flame boss, costume collapse and sheep reveal; one blue gem per hit (15 treasure total).

Both derive from the project's `Base_Enemy_BP` and require the enabled SpyroGameplay runtime module plus the existing Spyro damage, gem and checkpoint Blueprints. Saved meshes, materials, animation sequences and sounds ship in Content. Research tools, Blender, an emulator, the original disc and the external Scripts folder are not runtime dependencies. Checked-in editor binaries target Windows x64 UE4.27.2; other targets require compiling the included source. No production map is changed by this implementation.

## Placement

Place the capsule on blocking ground. Sleeping Dog remembers its starting transform, wakes when Spyro approaches, pounces, lands, returns home and sleeps. Ram is resisted through the existing enemy contract. The first flame plays the reaction geometry, then switches to the scorched geometry; the second defeats it. The shared dropper checkpoint event restores health, pose, position and collision. An explicit `Pursuer` is optional; the local player character is the fallback.

Toasty's `ToastyBehavior` component exposes three `StageLocations`, in **local centimetres**, relative to his starting transform. Defaults are `(0,0,0)`, `(900,0,0)`, `(1800,0,0)`. Rotate the actor to orient the route. Author these positions on connected, sufficiently wide ground. Movement uses terrain sweeps, supported footprint probes and short local detours around walls. Shallow wall overlap can recover to a nearby supported position. This is local steering rather than full navigation pathfinding; unsupported or enclosed routes still stop, so verify both retreat segments in your level.

Assign Sleeping Dog actors explicitly in `Guards`, giving each a stage number 0, 1 or 2. Each dog belongs to one stage. `ValidateEncounter` reports missing/wrong actors, duplicate assignments and invalid stage settings. Empty guard groups allow standalone combat. With living guards, Toasty holds his idle pose until Spyro approaches within `ApproachDistance` (440 cm by default), at a compatible height and with clear line of sight. He then keeps fleeing, even if Spyro backs away, inside `GuardAreaRadius` (730 cm by default) around the current stage anchor. Terrain probes select short detours around obstructions. Arrival at a new stage or checkpoint reset clears the approach trigger. Living dogs **do not make Toasty flame-immune**. Defeating the current stage's last dog releases Toasty to face and attack Spyro. Reset all encounter actors through the project's checkpoint system; resetting Toasty alone does not resurrect his dogs. No nearest-actor pairing or production-level actor names are required.

Toasty accepts one flame during each vulnerable phase. Hit reactions, transformation and retreat reject extra hits. The first hit sends him to stage 1. The second leaves the burned costume, plays the sheep reveal and sends the sheep to stage 2. The final hit plays the sheep defeat before releasing the final blue gem. The burned costume is hidden with the boss at final cleanup and on reset. Existing indexed gem identities and dropper bookkeeping are used, including permanent/pending collection filtering.

## Reference and adaptation boundaries

The reference is the NTSC level 14 overlay, with class 335 Sleeping Dog and class 314 Toasty. The pinned [spyro-1 assembly](https://github.com/TheMobyCollective/spyro-1/blob/46b51d3a5c34dd2585b4fe1de74ac9b74c76390d/asm/nonmatchings/overlays/level_14/func_level_14_8007AF94.s) was compared word-for-word against the local disc overlay: all 7,208 instructions in the listing matched. The overlay SHA-256 is `3b412fceb81e9ab60eef189fc3292387efae0886f4ed0ada0e169f9a190eb049`.

The components run 30 Hz simulation with interpolated presentation. They sample original frame indices instead of trusting the exported FBX's stretched clip durations. Dog supplies 16 clips across three skeletal variants; Toasty supplies 11 animated clips across four skeletal variants plus the static burned costume. Audio uses eight dog and ten boss samples extracted from the level, with original animation frame cue tables.

Original timing, frame counts, dog ballistic constants, attack phases, flame response and reward sequence inform the implementation. Coordinate conversion is 0.146104 centimetres per original world unit. Dog geometry uses scale 0.292208; Toasty's costume uses 0.584416 and the sheep uses 0.292208.

Unreal capsule sweeps, ground probes, player damage calls and line-of-sight checks replace the original collision engine. Outside the pounce, player blocking and flame queries use an oriented box fitted after each evaluated animation pose: all dog/sheep vertex joints, or Toasty's neck, robe, legs, pumpkin head and feet. The costume's broad hat, arms and scythe do not inflate his body hitbox. The charge sensor follows the same box with 1 cm padding and is registered as the native Damageable component's hitbox, including its single-hit blanket breath trace. The root capsule has collision disabled and is used explicitly for terrain sweeps; it cannot create a second oversized flame target. Dog pounce contact uses the original model's nonblocking collision policy, allowing descent through stationary or invincible Spyro. A separate source-derived capsule-like overlap requests shared Crush damage only when attack clips 2/9 select collision frames 10â€“18, including early grounded landing recovery. Current animation/frame is selected at zero interpolation progress; any positive progress selects the next animation/frame. The damaging shape retains the original core, spherical endcaps and broad bound, using a source-sized player sphere above the feet rather than the full animated body box. Rejected damage remains eligible later in that attack's active window. BP_Spyro uses its existing squashed pose, recovery and squash-death states; the shared player damage function retains health and invincibility handling. Broad flame/charge sensor coverage is retained. Ground projection settles the terrain capsule across slopes and triangle seams before movement. These volumes approximate the original model collision; they are not a port of its per-frame collision primitives. Toasty's active attack windows use the decoded model collision groups (costume clip 3 from frame 9, sheep clip 10 from frame 5). Explicit stage anchors and guard assignments replace original level indices and pod data; guarded movement is an adaptation around those anchors. Arbitrary level terrain, obstruction recovery and the UE checkpoint system therefore cannot be considered bit-identical to the original game.

## Physical contact and Blueprint feedback (3 October 2026)

Toasty's final scythe damage check now uses the same physical obstruction policy as Town Square enemies: solid WorldStatic/WorldDynamic/PhysicsBody/Destructible surfaces block contact even when they ignore Visibility, while overlap triggers and camera/Visibility-only geometry do not. Contact requires overlapping body-height intervals and ignores the combatants and their child actors. Existing attack distance, cone, height limits, animation windows and rejected-hit consumption policy remain in force. Awareness checks keep their existing rules.

The Sleeping Dog and Toasty components expose optional `OnEnemySignal` Blueprint feedback. Dogs report pounce commitment and native reset completion. Toasty additionally reports stage changes, observed last-guard defeat and chosen recovery detours. See [README.md](README.md) for exact Detail values and dispatch/lifecycle rules. No saved Blueprint rewiring is required. The Dog's source-derived pounce contact windows, first-flame color timing and airborne death handling remain unchanged.
